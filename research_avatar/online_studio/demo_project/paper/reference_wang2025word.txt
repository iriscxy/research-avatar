Word Form Matters studies how a controlled character-order perturbation changes semantic reconstruction while preserving the intended lexical identity. Its introduction begins with a concrete scrambled-word example, narrows the unresolved mechanism, and states bounded contributions.

The related-work section separates empirical robustness observations from representational explanations. Claims about prior systems remain distinct from claims tested in the current experiment.

The method introduces notation only after the task is defined, gives each quantity an operational interpretation, and connects formal objects to the hypotheses later tested in experiments.

The experimental section first fixes data, models, and metrics, then states the main comparison, and finally analyzes a mechanism-specific result. Limitations distinguish evidence from extrapolation.

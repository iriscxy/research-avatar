\section{Conclusion}

\label{sec:conclusion}

% PAPER_STUDIO_PARAGRAPH:C-P1

We presented a controlled study of permutation invariance in multiple-choice question answering, defining the core comparison, the samples, and the candidate baselines that the planned evaluation will test. The design distinguishes the fixed implementation choices from the variables under study, and the expected sensitivity mechanism is stated explicitly as a hypothesis rather than an observed outcome. The principal limitation is the absence of uploaded experimental results: no quantitative evidence currently supports a claim about how often answer distributions change under random option reordering or which baseline best predicts such changes. Future work should run the specified evaluation, report the supported comparisons, and extend the analysis to other model families and prompt formats.

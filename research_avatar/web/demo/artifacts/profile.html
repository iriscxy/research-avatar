Core voice and abstract blueprint

The voice is direct, technical, and consequence-oriented. It prefers a concrete phenomenon over a generic motivation, and a named mechanism over a vague “novel framework.” A reusable abstract arc is: (1) establish the deployed capability or scientific phenomenon; (2) identify the evaluation or explanatory blind spot; (3) reframe the problem with one central object; (4) name the dataset, metric, framework, or hypothesis; (5) explain two or three load-bearing components; (6) report the decisive cross-model, cross-domain, causal, or theoretical evidence; (7) state the practical implication or release. This is a measured tendency, not a mandatory sentence template.

Introduction architecture

  1. Paragraph 1 — stakes. Begin with the capability in use and why its failure matters. Most papers use a compact landscape opening; wang2025under is the notable narrative variant, opening with the Babel story before converting it into a testable linguistic-relativity question.
  2. Paragraph 2 — precise bottleneck. Contrast current progress with what is still unmeasured, mechanistically unclear, over-simplified, static, or capacity-limited.
  3. Paragraph 3 — evidence-backed reframing. Introduce the observable or formal object: safety boundary, concept alignment, feature inversion, emotion circuit, information capacity, or cylindrical geometry.
  4. Paragraph 4 — approach. Name the method and describe its modules in causal order, not implementation order.
  5. Final introduction paragraph — contributions. Use a short numbered or bulleted set when there are separable artifact, finding, and method claims; each item should pair the contribution with its evidence.

Section organization and Related Work

The modal body order is Introduction → Related Work → named dataset/mechanism/framework → Experiments → Conclusion. Related Work is usually an early thematic §2 with two to four conceptual clusters, not a chronology. Theory-forward papers may delay it: wan2025fano puts formalization, bound, challenge anatomy, framework, and experiments before Related Work. Dataset papers give the artifact its own high-level section and move from construction to statistics/quality checks to downstream evaluation. Appendices are substantial and routinely carry prompts, proofs, extra datasets, qualitative cases, and reproducibility detail.

Method, notation, and naming conventions

  • Introduce the central noun in plain language before formalization, then assign a memorable name or acronym: ABD, AJailBench/APT, DyFlow, BiasLens, StyloBench/StyloCheck, CRH.
  • Organize the method as subsection-per-module when modules have different causal roles; describe inputs/outputs and purpose before equations.
  • Use notation to expose a bottleneck or decomposition, not to decorate an empirical story. Definitions should support a later bound, metric, intervention, or ablation.
  • Prefer mechanism names that encode the explanatory picture (“safety boundary,” “feature-inversion trap,” “cylindrical representation”) and artifact names that remain pronounceable.

Experiment narrative and result paragraphs

Experiments usually progress from construct validity to main comparison to mechanism/ablation to robustness or transfer. Result paragraphs lead with the observed pattern (“performance collapses,” “attention concentrates,” “the direction reverses”), point to the exact table/figure, quantify the difference, then interpret it against the proposed mechanism. Strong papers close the loop by testing a prediction implied by the explanation—not merely showing that a new method wins. Baselines are grouped by family, and benchmark papers emphasize coverage and difficulty strata before leaderboard values.

Figures, tables, and captions

  • Figure 1 is usually conceptual and argumentative: dataset/pipeline overview, old-vs-new paradigm, or mechanism geometry. It should let the reader predict the rest of the paper.
  • Method figures use labeled stages and directional flow; theory figures pair a geometric schematic with an empirical curve or heatmap.
  • Tables are dense and comparative, with metric direction, grouped baselines, and visual emphasis on best/second-best results.
  • Captions are semi-self-contained: they say what is compared, decode panels or colors, and often include the intended takeaway. Avoid captions that merely repeat the title.

Paragraph and sentence mechanics

Paragraphs typically open with a claim or transition, then narrow through evidence. Common moves are “However,” “To address this,” “Specifically,” “Furthermore,” and “Overall.” Sentences can carry multiple clauses when specifying a pipeline, but rhetorical jobs remain explicit. First-person plural is normal for contributions and experimental actions. Claims are confident at the artifact level and qualified at the generalization level. Define acronyms once, avoid switching names, and use parenthetical examples to make an abstract concept concrete.

Limitations, ethics, and conclusion patterns

Conclusions are short: restate the named artifact or mechanism, summarize two or three findings, and point to the scientific or deployment consequence. Limitations are often a dedicated section or appendix adjacent to societal impact; they should name coverage limits (models, languages, modalities, domains), evaluation dependence, and foreseeable misuse. Safety and benchmark work should distinguish diagnostic release value from attack enablement. Future work is narrow and mechanism-linked rather than a generic expansion list.

Contribution-type variants

Engineering

Lead with rigidity or deployment failure; describe the module chain; prove generalization across tasks; pair gains with efficiency, safety, or capability-retention costs.

Theory

Lead with an empirical contradiction; define one central object; state the bound/hypothesis; derive predictions; validate geometry or curves; separate predictable from intrinsically uncertain behavior.

Benchmark

Lead with evaluation blind spots; specify coverage axes and construction controls; validate quality; reveal model capability boundaries; release the artifact and warn against reading a single aggregate score.

Anti-imitation safeguards

Match the structural habits—gap precision, named central object, diagnostic-to-intervention loop, and evidence cadence—without copying prior sentences, metaphors, acronyms, contribution lists, or caption wording. Do not transplant “first” claims. Re-measure every number, cite the actual source, and let the new paper’s evidence determine whether the opening is landscape-first, phenomenon-first, or theory-first.

Drafting checklist

  • Can the paper’s boundary or contradiction be stated in one sentence?
  • Is there one named central object, and does each module serve it?
  • Does Figure 1 communicate the argument before implementation detail?
  • Does every contribution item point to a table, figure, proof, dataset, or release?
  • Do result paragraphs state observation → magnitude → mechanism → implication?
  • Are robustness, transfer, cost, and capability retention tested where relevant?
  • Are priority and generalization claims scoped; are limitations concrete?
  • Has phrasing been checked against the 15-paper corpus to avoid sentence reuse?

1 · Literature Landscape

The MoE field in 2024–2026 has converged on one architecture and fractured on one question. The architecture is fine-grained experts + a few always-on shared experts + top-k / sigmoid routing (DeepSeekMoE → DeepSeek-V3, OLMoE, Qwen3-MoE, which drops the shared expert). Load balancing has shifted from an auxiliary-loss gradient toward aux-loss-free bias-based balancing, because the balance-loss gradient measurably hurts quality — but the balance-vs-specialization tension is fundamental and implementation-sensitive. Discrete top-k remains the pain point, spawning differentiable and router-free escapes, none of which has displaced learned top-k in production.

The fracture is what experts actually specialize in. One camp finds robust fine-grained specialization — POS-sensitive routers, semantic routing recoverable by linear probes, monosemantic-by-design experts. A rising counter-camp argues the apparent specialization is a hidden-state geometry artifact of the linear router, and that a large "domain-invariant standing committee" of always-active experts dominates routing. This unresolved tension is the single most exploitable seam for a white-box researcher.

For a trustworthy-LLM / steering researcher, the most actionable findings cluster around safety localization: SteerMoE shows expert (de)activation steers safety (+20%) and faithfulness (+27%) with no weight change — but the same handle is an attack. SAFEx finds refusal is dangerously concentrated: disabling 12 of 6,144 experts in Qwen3-30B-A3B drops the refusal rate 22%. SafeMoE defends harmful fine-tuning by penalizing routing drift — but requires the original safety-aligned routing weights, unavailable in most deployments. The offense side ( buffer overflow, prompt-stealing via tie-break leakage, BadMoE dormant-expert backdoors, input-only routing jailbreaks) is far more developed than defense, and the router — not the experts — is the recurring attack surface. There is still no deployment-realistic (black-box, no reference-weights) defense, and MoE-specific faithfulness/trust evaluation is thin.

Two more seams matter for her profile. Cross-lingual / domain knowledge localizes to experts: deactivating ~20 of 6,000 experts erases correct answers in >40% of cases, and domain-specific experts demonstrably exist — but repair, not just localization, is unaddressed. And RL post-training on MoE is the least-mature sub-angle: GRPO-style RL collapses because the router differs between rollout and training (R3, RSPO are corrective patches, not RL-co-designed routers). On the cheap-construction side, sparse upcycling produces homogeneous experts and LoRA-as-experts is the cheapest MoE she can build on one GPU.

Structural gaps → idea seeds: (1) safety concentration has attacks but no redundancy-based defense that works without reference routing weights; (2) knowledge is localizable per expert/language but not repaired; (3) upcycled experts are homogeneous / dormant and no one uses small specialist teachers to revive them; (4) MoE-specific trust/faithfulness eval under routing shift is missing; (5) the semantic-vs-geometric specialization question is unresolved and causally testable.

3 · Idea Cards

RANK 1 · I1  novel ✅ SELECTED
Redistribute refusal behavior across many experts (LoRA + routing regularizer) so no small ablatable expert subset can be knocked out to jailbreak the model — a defense against expert-ablation attacks that needs no original aligned routing weights.
Contribution type: method (defense)
Fit: Trustworthy/Safe LLMs (stated #1) + steering
Feasibility: HIGH — LoRA fine-tune on Qwen3-30B-A3B or OLMoE; inference-only ablation eval; single A100 w/ offloading, or 4090 w/ smaller MoE
Risk: MED
Rough effort: ~5–7 weeks
Reviewer's likely objection: "You made SAFEx harder — but did deployable safety actually improve under adaptive attacks, and at what over-refusal cost?"

Method steps

  • Reproduce the attack (cheap, first — W1): replicate SAFEx/SteerMoE worst-case k-expert ablation on Qwen3-30B-A3B (& OLMoE) using her jailbreak-benchmark harness; record refusal-rate drop vs k as the baseline vulnerability curve.
  • Redistribution objective: LoRA fine-tune with a routing regularizer that (a) spreads safety-relevant routing mass across many experts and (b) adds an entropy/anti-concentration term on the safety-critical expert set — so refusal survives ablation of any small subset.
  • Adaptive re-attack: re-run worst-case ablation and input-only routing jailbreaks against the hardened model (attacker re-optimizes k against the new routing) — the honest evaluation, not just the static one.
  • Utility & over-refusal guardrail: track general capability + over-refusal on benign prompts to bound the safety tax.

Hypothesis / acceptance: after hardening, the worst-case refusal drop at the SAFEx budget (≈12 experts) falls from ~22% to <5%, and survives adaptive re-optimization, with <2% general-capability loss and no significant over-refusal rise.

MVE (minimum viable experiment): one model (Qwen3-30B-A3B), one jailbreak set, reproduce the SAFEx drop, apply the regularizer, show the ablation curve flattens — before scaling to a second model / adaptive attacker.

Novelty vs her own work: extends her trustworthiness / Safety-Boundaries / jailbreak-benchmark line from attack & evaluation into architectural defense — new territory, not a restatement.

Closest work & differentiation: SAFEx and SteerMoE expose the concentration/ablation vulnerability but propose no redistribution defense; SafeMoE defends harmful-FT but requires the original aligned routing weights — I1's redistribution needs none, and targets ablation attacks rather than fine-tuning drift.

RANK 2 · I3  novel
Use cross-lingual inconsistency to localize the experts holding wrong/missing medical knowledge in a target language, then repair just those experts with targeted per-expert LoRA — turning known localization into an actual fix, on her CURE-Med stack.
Contribution type: method (repair)
Fit: Multilingual/medical LLMs + CURE-Med
Feasibility: HIGH — per-expert LoRA is parameter-light; reuses CURE-Med data/eval; single A100
Risk: MED
Rough effort: ~6–8 weeks
Reviewer's likely objection: "Is targeted expert repair actually better than ordinary multilingual/domain LoRA at equal parameter budget?"

Method steps

  • Localize (cheap, first): compute cross-lingual answer-inconsistency on medical QA per expert (XICI-style) to identify the small expert set whose deactivation erases correct answers in the weak language.
  • Targeted repair: attach per-expert LoRA only to the localized experts and fine-tune on the medical curriculum in the weak language, freezing the rest — the "surgical" alternative to whole-model multilingual tuning.
  • Router-usage check: verify the router keeps selecting the repaired experts post-repair (the known failure mode) and doesn't route around them.
  • Equal-budget baseline (W7): compare against standard full-model / random-expert LoRA at matched trainable-parameter budget — the objection killer.

Hypothesis / acceptance: targeted repair recovers weak-language medical accuracy to within X% of the strong language, beating equal-budget non-targeted LoRA, without degrading the strong language.

MVE: one MoE, one weak↔strong language pair, one medical QA set — show localization → repair beats matched-budget baseline before adding languages/domains.

Novelty vs her own work: CURE-Med does multilingual medical curriculum-RL on (implicitly dense) models; I3 makes the MoE structure the lever — localize-then-repair is a new mechanism, not a re-run of CURE-Med.

Closest work & differentiation: cross-lingual knowledge localization [2026 preprint — verify id before citing] localizes but does not repair; domain-expert existence audits [2026 preprint — verify] confirm experts exist but propose only training-free steering, not knowledge repair.

RANK 3 · I6  novel ✅ SELECTED
Flip her "small models teach LLMs" KD: use small domain-specialist models as teachers to specialize the dormant/underused experts of a cheaply-upcycled MoE, breaking upcycling homogeneity so the router actually uses them.
Contribution type: method (KD / expert-utilization)
Fit: KD (Flipping-KD) + cheap MoE construction
Feasibility: HIGH — LoRA-as-experts / small upcycled MoE; small teachers are cheap; single A100/4090
Risk: MED
Rough effort: ~5–7 weeks
Reviewer's likely objection: "Plausible — but does the router actually use the revived experts, and does end-task utility improve (vs load-balancing faking specialization)?"

Method steps

  • Scope small (W1): build a small upcycled MoE (or LoRA-experts / MixLoRA) from a Qwen3 dense checkpoint; measure per-expert utilization to identify dormant experts.
  • Small-teacher assignment: pair each dormant expert with a small domain-specialist teacher and distill the teacher's behavior into that expert (her Flipping-KD direction: small expertise → large model).
  • Router incentive: add a light routing term so the router is induced to actually route the target domain to the revived expert.
  • Utilization + utility eval: report the change in expert-utilization entropy and downstream task gains — both, so the win isn't just cosmetic balancing.

Hypothesis / acceptance: small-teacher revival raises dormant-expert utilization and yields a measurable downstream gain over (a) vanilla upcycling and (b) dense-KD, at equal compute.

MVE: one small upcycled MoE, 2–3 domains, show dormant→active + a task gain vs vanilla upcycling before scaling.

Novelty vs her own work: directly leverages her Flipping-KD identity (small models' expertise → LLMs), applied to a new object — MoE expert revival — rather than text matching.

Closest work & differentiation: Every Expert Matters does dense→MoE KD aggregating all experts; I6 flips the direction (small specialist teachers) and targets dormant experts specifically; Drop-Upcycling breaks homogeneity by re-init, not by distilled specialization.

1 · Literature Landscape

The direction sits on your intellectual center (CRH → Emotion Circuits → BiasLens → ABD). The first pass surveyed steering methods, reliable/geometry-aware steering, circuit discovery/editing, and the reliability audits (see the appendix, categories A–F). This pass adds the engineering front you asked for: turning steering from a fragile inference-time hack into TRAINED, deployable behavior control (appendix category G).

The trained/deployable end of steering is real but fragmented. Trained steering-as-a-module is built — ReFT/LoReFT trains interventions on frozen activations as a PEFT drop-in, Representation Tuning bakes a steering direction straight into weights via an activation-matching loss, and RePS trains a robustness-hardened preference-steering objective. Composition is built but shallow — LoraHub and LoRA-on-the-Go compose/route adapters, but the recurring failure is interference and no one ships a behavior-adapter suite with measured non-interference. Steering-as-RL-reward is emerging — RLIF/Intuitor and Small Vectors, Big Effects — but target reasoning, not robust behavior control, and reward-hack. And steering-in-serving already exists — EasySteer and vLLM Hook v0 make steering a vLLM serving hook.

The load-bearing negative result — the seed for the engineering flagship. Shifting Perspectives shows that naive LoRA did not converge on a robust behavior subspace: baking steering into weights LOSES the adversarial/shift robustness that inference-time steering had. A distillation objective that preserves that robustness is unbuilt.

Three engineering white spaces (all buildable on your Qwen3 + TRL/PEFT/vLLM/DeepSpeed stack):

  • EG1 — robustness-preserving distillation. Nobody bakes steering into weights while keeping inference-steering's robustness under distribution shift / adaptive attack (the Shifting Perspectives gap). → I8.
  • EG2 — a reliability gate in the loop. Extraction, conditional gating (CAST), distillation, and serving (EasySteer) exist in isolation; nothing chains them behind a pass/FAIL reliability gate that rejects unreliable steers before deployment. → I9.
  • EG3 — measured behavioral non-interference. Adapter composition exists but interference is documented, not guaranteed; no behavior-adapter suite ships a measured pairwise non-interference. → I10 (the buildable form of the carried I4).

3 · Idea Cards

I8 · Robustness-preserving steering distillation novel 🆕 new this run

Bake behavior steering into the weights with a training objective explicitly designed to PRESERVE the distribution-shift and adversarial robustness that inference-time steering has but naive LoRA-baking loses — turning fragile activation hacks into a deployable, robust-by-training control, benchmarked under attack and shift.
Novelty7/10. Closest: Representation Tuning (bakes steering into weights, no robustness focus), RePS (trained robust preference steering — resists prompt-jailbreaks), Small Vectors, Big Effects (RL-trained steering vectors). Differentiation: the explicit target is the Shifting Perspectives negative result — preserve inference-steering's shift/adversarial robustness during distillation, with attack+shift benchmarks and honest negatives. The cleanest research-shaped idea: it attacks a known failure mode, not "another steering method." FeasibilityHigh — your TRL/PEFT/RL + DeepSpeed stack; Qwen3-4B/7B. RiskMED-HI — the null is plausible: weight-baking may still lose robustness, or robustness trades off with control/utility. Either outcome is a publishable, decision-useful result. ContributionMethod + empirical (targets a field negative result). FitYour steering line + RL-tuning stack (One Model / DyFlow). Reviewer's objection"This is Representation Tuning + adversarial regularization — why should it generalize?" → answer with attack+shift benchmarks vs inference-steering, ReFT, RePS, and a mechanistic account of why the objective preserves robustness.
Method steps
  1. Extract steering directions for target behaviors (refusal, sentiment, formality) with your usual contrastive pipeline.
  2. Distill into weights via activation-matching + a robustness regularizer that matches inference-steering behavior under perturbed / shifted / adversarial inputs (optionally an RL objective with a robustness reward).
  3. Benchmark control + utility + robustness under distribution shift + adaptive attacks vs inference-steering, ReFT, Representation Tuning, RePS, and naive LoRA-baking.
  4. Report the robustness-vs-controllability frontier honestly (the negative-result axis).
Hypothesis / acceptance: the robustness-preserving objective retains most of inference-steering's under-shift/attack robustness while removing the inference-time hook, beating naive LoRA-baking at matched control.
MVE: one behavior (refusal), robustness under 5 paraphrase/attack sets: distilled vs inference-steering vs naive LoRA — one node, ~2 days.
vs your own work: new — targets a field negative result, not a restatement of ABD/Emotion Circuits. Pairs with I9 (reviewer's recommended build).

I9 · Reliability-gated auto steering-vector factory differentiable 🆕 new this run

An end-to-end pipeline that turns a natural-language behavior description into a served control adapter — mining the direction, gating it CAST-style, running a pass/FAIL reliability check, and distilling only reliable behaviors into the serving stack, so unreliable steers are rejected before deployment.
Novelty6/10. Closest: EasySteer (serving + vector library, no reliability gate), CAST (gating), Persona Vectors (auto extraction). Differentiation: the reliability GATE that predicts deployment failure + end-to-end chaining. Risk of reading as glue — the gate must beat existing in-distribution evals at predicting real failure. FeasibilityHigh — chains existing components on your stack; this is the buildable realization of the carried I1/I7 reliability ideas. RiskMED-HI — the gate may overfit easy evals; most useful behaviors may fail the gate; distillation may break a gated behavior. ContributionSystem + reliability science. FitYour evaluation/probing line + serving. Reviewer's objection"System-integration paper — where's the science?" → the science is the predictive reliability gate (predicts post-deployment failure better than control accuracy), not the plumbing.
Method: (1) NL behavior → synth contrastive data → extract direction; (2) CAST-style conditional gate; (3) reliability check (off-target + shift + jailbreak) → pass/FAIL; (4) distill reliable ones into a served adapter (vLLM/EasySteer). Ship a toolkit.
Hypothesis: the reliability gate predicts post-deployment failure better than in-distribution control accuracy. MVE: 5 behaviors through the pipeline; gate's predicted-vs-actual failure correlation. Best paired with I8.

I10 · Behavior-adapter library with measured non-interference differentiable 🆕 new this run

Distill steering directions for a set of behaviors (safety, formality, emotion, honesty) into composable LoRA/ReFT adapters, shipped as a suite with a MEASURED pairwise non-interference guarantee, so they can be stacked blindly at serve time.

I2 · Closed-loop discover→edit→verify circuit controller differentiable

A feedback controller that discovers a behavior circuit, applies a graded edit, and verifies target-control + off-target + fluency online — accepting the edit only when a specificity constraint passes, replacing today's hand-tuned control strength.
Novelty6/10. Real gap (no closed controller), differentiable-from-glue only with a principled specificity constraint + search policy. Discovery (EAP/feature circuits) and eval (Off-a-Cliff) exist separately. FeasibilityMED — engineering-heavy; reuse your emotion-circuit discovery as the localizer. RiskHIGH — verifier gameable; circuit discovery noisy for stable closed-loop control. ContributionMethod / systems. FitYour circuit-control + DyFlow controller instincts.
Method: localize circuit (EAP/feature circuits) → graded edit → online verifier (target + held-out off-target + fluency) → feedback search over strength/locus until specificity passes. The most engineering of the carried theory ideas.

I4 · Compositional non-interference for behavioral steering novel

Edit one behavior's circuit while constraining the edit to the null space of other behaviors' directions, so you can move emotion without moving truthfulness or refusal — porting AlphaEdit's factual null-space idea to behavioral steering.
Novelty7/10. Real gap. Closest: AlphaEdit / AlphaSteer — null-space applied to factual editing / refusal, never to compositional multi-attribute behavioral control. I10 is its buildable, trained realization. FeasibilityMED. RiskHIGH — behavior directions non-orthogonal/entangled; null constraints may erase target efficacy. ContributionMethod. FitYour steering + emotion-circuit line.
Method: extract directions/circuits for K behaviors → constrain each edit to the null space of the others → measure the interference matrix vs additive multi-vector. Choose I4 for the principled version, I10 for the shipped-adapter version.

I1 · Sensitive-Sector Steering (the science option) novel

Instead of committing to the difference-vector direction, empirically estimate the causally-sensitive sub-region of a concept's normal plane (the CRH "sensitive sector") and steer along the per-sample, sector-corrected direction — turning your CRH's diagnosis of steering instability into an actual fix.
Novelty8/10. The survey is explicit: no method identifies the sensitive sector at all (Collateral-Damage uses only a global anisotropy prior). The most novel idea overall — but the most theory-flavored, which is why it dropped after your redirect. FeasibilityHigh — inference-time; reuse CRH machinery. RiskMED — CRH says the sector is intrinsically uncertain; estimates may be unstable. ContributionMethod + diagnostic. FitDirect follow-up to your CRH.
Method: compute central axis + normal plane (CRH) → in-plane causal-effect probe to locate the sensitive sector → per-sample sector-corrected steer → benchmark control + off-target + outcome variance vs CAA/Spherical/Collateral-Damage. Pick this only if you want the science paper (pairs with I7).

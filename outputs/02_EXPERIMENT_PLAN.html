1 · Projected Abstract (write FIRST)

PROJECTED — not results. In your gap-first style; no em-dashes; placeholders only.
Jailbreak defenses for large language models are usually evaluated offline and on text alone, and the strongest activation level defense, Activation Boundary Defense, still refuses too many benign prompts and has never been tested as a deployed system or against audio attacks. We present ABD++, a serve time jailbreak defense that fuses activation boundary constraints with input conditional steering: a lightweight detector fires a rejection steer only when an input is judged harmful, so benign prompts pass untouched. We build ABD++ as a vLLM serving hook and measure it under a fixed latency budget. On text attacks ABD++ reaches [X%] defense success rate, above ABD and steering baselines, while cutting over refusal to [X%] and keeping utility within [X%] of the base model. We further show ABD++ transfers to large audio language models, lowering attack success rate on AJailBench by [X%]. Under attack aware adaptive attacks its defense success rate drops only [X%], far less than static defenses. We release the defense and its text and audio evaluation suite.

Introduction (one topic sentence per paragraph, gap-first)

  • ¶1 — Jailbreaks remain the central safety risk for deployed LLMs, and defenses are judged by how much they block versus how much they break benign use.
  • ¶2 — Activation-level defenses (your ABD) give the strongest block rates, but they are always-on, so they over-refuse benign prompts, and they have only been shown offline on text.
  • ¶3 — Two gaps follow: (i) the defense should fire only when needed (conditional), and (ii) it must survive as a real deployed system (latency), across modalities (audio), and against an adaptive attacker.
  • ¶4 — We present ABD++, fusing the boundary constraint with conditional rejection steering on a vLLM hook, and evaluate it on text + audio + adaptive attacks; contributions as enumerated bullets ("We propose / We build / We release").

Related Work (sections + one-line position)

  • 2.1 Jailbreak attacks — optimization (GCG, AutoDAN), model-based (PAIR, ReNeLLM), and audio (AJailBench); we defend against all three families.
  • 2.2 Inference-time defenses — decoding (SmoothLLM, ROSE/Self-CD), steering (AdaSteer, CAST, AlphaSteer), and activation-boundary (ABD); we fuse boundary + conditional steering, which none combine.
  • 2.3 Steering-in-serving — EasySteer / vLLM Hook make steering deployable; we build the first activation-boundary defense as a serving hook with a latency budget.

Method — modules

  • 3.1 Activation Boundary Constraint — your ABD: penalize outlier activation coordinates on BO-selected low/middle layers (recap + reuse).
  • 3.2 Conditional Rejection Steering — a CAST-style condition detector fires a rejection-direction steer only on inputs judged harmful, cutting over-refusal.
  • 3.3 Fusion & vLLM Serving Hook — order + interaction of 3.1 and 3.2 inside a vLLM hook under a latency budget.
  • 3.4 Audio Extension — apply the fused defense to LAM activations (Qwen2-Audio) for AJailBench.
